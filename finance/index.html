The legal minimum from 1 April 2026, plus the voluntary benchmarks that help you keep good carers.

This is a hard legal floor. You must also pay for all working time — travel between visits and paid training count. Paying the right hourly rate but not paying travel time can still breach the minimum wage, which HMRC actively investigates in home care.

Tax and payroll, in plain English

The general picture for a small care employer. This is guidance, not regulated accountancy advice — have an accountant set up payroll and confirm your figures. VAT and the Employment Allowance genuinely need their eyes on your numbers.

What you deduct from wages and send to HMRC

  • Income tax (PAYE) on pay above the £12,570 personal allowance.
  • Employee National Insurance.

These come out of the carer's wage, not yours. You collect and pass them to HMRC.

What you pay on top of wages (your cost)

  • Employer NI: 15% on each person's earnings above £5,000 a year (£417/month).
  • Employment Allowance: can cut your employer NI bill by up to £10,500 a year. You need at least one employee (or a second director) paid above £5,000 — a sole-director company can't claim. Watch-point for your accountant: it can be restricted if most of your work is for the public sector (e.g. mostly council-funded packages).
  • Under-21s and apprentices under 25 attract no employer NI on most earnings.
  • Workplace pension: once you employ staff you must enrol eligible workers (earnings trigger £10,000). Minimum 8% of qualifying earnings (£6,240–£50,270): employer at least 3%, employee 5%. NEST is free to set up.

The company's own taxes

  • Corporation Tax: 19% on profits up to £50,000, 25% above £250,000, with marginal relief between. Confirm current rates each year.
  • VAT — the important one for your mixed business: regulated personal care by a CQC-registered provider is VAT exempt. But consultancy and training (Care Hub) are standard-rated at 20%. If that standard-rated turnover passes the £90,000 threshold you must register, which brings "partial exemption" rules. You're likely below it now — ask an accountant when to register, and don't wait until you're already over.
  • Filing: annual accounts and a Confirmation Statement to Companies House, and a Corporation Tax return to HMRC.

Do your own bookkeeping (Xero, QuickBooks or FreeAgent), but pay a small-practice accountant (~£500–£1,500/year) for the year-end accounts, Corporation Tax return and VAT timing. It's inexpensive and prevents costly mistakes.
